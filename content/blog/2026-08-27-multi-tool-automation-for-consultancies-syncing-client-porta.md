If you run a consultancy, you already know the feeling: a client emails to ask about their project status, their invoice is sitting unpaid in your accounting tool, their deliverables are uploaded to a portal that nobody remembered to update, and somehow you're the one manually connecting all these dots at 6pm on a Friday. The work itself isn't complicated — it's just relentless, repetitive, and invisible to everyone except you. This is exactly the kind of glue work that AI automation was built to eliminate, and consultancies are one of the best-placed businesses to benefit from it.

## The Real Cost of Disconnected Tools

Most consultancies run on a familiar stack: a client portal (like Clinked or Huddle), an email platform (Outlook or Gmail), a project management tool (Asana, Monday, or ClickUp), and an invoicing or accounting system (Xero, QuickBooks, or FreshBooks). Each of these tools does its job reasonably well in isolation. The problem is the space between them.

Every time a project milestone is hit, someone has to remember to update the client portal, trigger the invoice, and send the status email. Miss one step and you get a confused client chasing an update, a delayed payment, or a deliverable sitting in a folder nobody knows about. Research from McKinsey estimates that knowledge workers spend around 20% of their working week on internal coordination and information-sharing tasks — for a five-person consultancy, that's roughly one full-time equivalent doing nothing but admin.

The fix isn't hiring another operations person. It's building an AI agent that sits between your tools and handles the hand-offs automatically.

## How AI Agents Handle the Glue Work

An AI agent, in plain terms, is a piece of software that can watch for triggers across your tools, make simple decisions based on rules or context, and take actions — without you having to initiate each one manually. Think of it as a very attentive operations coordinator who never sleeps and never forgets a step.

Here's what a connected automation looks like in practice for a consultancy:

1. **Project milestone completed** (logged in Asana or Monday) → AI agent detects the trigger
2. **Client portal updated** automatically with the relevant deliverable or status note
3. **Invoice generated and sent** via Xero or QuickBooks for that milestone
4. **Client notified** by a personalised email summarising what's been completed and what comes next

None of those four steps require a human to initiate them. The agent handles the sequencing, checks that each step completed successfully, and flags any exceptions (like a missing client email address or a portal upload failure) for a human to review.

Tools like Zapier, Make (formerly Integromat), and n8n can handle the basic workflow plumbing. Layering in an LLM — a large language model, which is the same technology behind ChatGPT — means the email your client receives isn't a stiff template. It reads like something a thoughtful account manager actually wrote, referencing the specific project, milestone name, and next steps.

## A Real-World Example: A Strategy Consultancy Cuts Admin Time by 40%

Consider a ten-person strategy consultancy running around 25 active client engagements at any one time. Before automation, their project lead estimated she was spending roughly eight hours a week just on milestone communications, portal updates, and chasing invoice approvals internally. That's a full working day — every week — on tasks that generated zero billable value.

After implementing a multi-tool automation connecting ClickUp, their client portal, and Xero, that eight hours dropped to under five hours per week in the first month, and closer to three hours by month three once edge cases were ironed out. At a blended rate of £85 per hour for senior staff time, that's a saving of approximately £21,000 per year — from a single automated workflow. The setup cost, using a specialist agency, came in at around £3,500.

The client experience improved too. Because portal updates and status emails now went out within minutes of a milestone being marked complete (rather than whenever someone remembered), clients reported feeling better informed. Two clients specifically mentioned it during renewal conversations as a reason they chose to continue the engagement.

## Practical Considerations Before You Build

Before you go looking for the "automate everything" button, it's worth thinking through a few things that will save you headaches later.

**Map your current process first.** Write down, step by step, what actually happens when a project milestone is reached. Who does what? What tools do they touch? Where do things fall through the cracks? You can't automate a process you haven't documented — or you'll just automate the broken version of it.

**Decide where humans stay in the loop.** Not every step should be fully automated. Invoices over a certain value, or clients flagged as complex, might warrant a human review before anything goes out. Build approval steps into your workflow from the start rather than retrofitting them after an awkward client moment.

**Check your tool integrations.** Most modern SaaS tools have APIs — essentially, connection points that allow other software to talk to them. Before committing to a workflow design, confirm that your specific portal, CRM, and accounting tools are supported by whichever automation platform you use. Zapier supports thousands of apps; Make and n8n offer more flexibility for custom connections.

**Start with one workflow, not five.** The milestone-to-invoice-to-email sequence is a good starting point because it has a clear trigger, clear outputs, and clear value. Get that working reliably before you automate client onboarding, meeting scheduling, and everything else simultaneously.

One thing worth noting: AI-generated emails should always have a consistent review cadence early on. Set a recurring calendar task for the first six to eight weeks to spot-check a sample of outgoing communications. The outputs are usually excellent, but you want to catch any tone mismatches or context errors before a client does.

## Conclusion

The consultancy model is built on selling expertise and relationships — not admin hours. Every minute your senior people spend manually updating portals, generating invoices, and drafting status emails is a minute not spent on client work, business development, or thinking. Multi-tool AI automation doesn't replace your team; it removes the invisible tax on their time that compounds across every active engagement. The technology to connect your client portal, email, and invoicing system already exists, the integration platforms are mature, and the ROI — as the example above shows — can be substantial within the first year. The main thing standing between you and a smoother operation is taking the time to map the process and then build the workflow that runs it for you.
